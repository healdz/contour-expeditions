Mountaineering Ecuador

The Avenue of the Volcanoes

1.47°S 78.82°WMax elev. 6,263 m14 Days
Dates & Prices View Itinerary
Overview

Ecuador's 'Avenue of the Volcanoes' is the ideal classroom for high-altitude mountaineering. This expedition builds carefully: acclimatization hikes above Quito, a glacier skills day, then guided attempts on Cayambe (5,790 m) and Cotopaxi (5,897 m) before the grand finale — Chimborazo (6,263 m), whose summit is the point on Earth closest to the sun. Nights are spent in historic haciendas and high mountain refuges.

Day by Day

Trip Itinerary

Day 1Arrive in Quito2,850 m

Transfer to a hotel in Quito's colonial centre. Evening briefing and gear check.

Day 2Rucu Pichincha4,698 m

Ride the TelefériQo and hike to the summit of Rucu Pichincha above the city — our first acclimatization objective.

Day 3Fuya Fuya and Otavalo4,263 m

Hike above the Mojanda lakes, then visit Otavalo's market. Overnight at a historic hacienda.

Day 4Cayambe refuge and glacier school4,600 m

Drive to the Cayambe refuge. Afternoon crampon, ice-axe, and rope-team skills on the glacier.

Day 5Summit attempt: Cayambe5,790 m

Alpine start for the highest point on the equator. Descend to the hacienda for a hot shower.

Day 6Rest: Papallacta hot springs3,300 m

Recover in the thermal pools of Papallacta.

Day 7Cotopaxi National Park3,600 m

Transfer to a hacienda on the edge of the park. Acclimatization walk on the páramo.

Day 8José Rivas refuge4,800 m

Hike to the refuge and review technique for summit night.

Day 9Summit attempt: Cotopaxi5,897 m

Climb one of the world's highest active volcanoes, with sunrise views over the Andes.

Day 10Rest at the hacienda3,600 m

Recovery day: horseback riding optional.

Day 11Chimborazo refuge4,850 m

Drive south past Riobamba to the Carrel refuge. Short acclimatization hike.

Day 12Summit attempt: Chimborazo6,263 m

The expedition's crux: a long, demanding climb to the summit farthest from Earth's centre.

Day 13Weather reserve day

Built-in buffer for weather or a second summit attempt.

Day 14Return to Quito and depart

Drive back to Quito for onward flights.

Common Questions

How fit do I need to be?

This is our most demanding trip. You should have multi-day hiking experience at altitude and be able to move for 10–12 hours on summit days. Prior crampon experience is helpful but not required — we teach glacier skills on day 4.

Can I join solo?

Absolutely — most of our guests travel solo. We pair solo travellers into shared rooms or tents, or you can add a single supplement.

What's included in the trip?

See the full list under What's Included above. In short: ifmga / aseguim-certified mountain guides (2:1 ratio on summit days); group technical gear: ropes, protection, and radios; glacier skills training day. Flights and travel insurance are not included.

What happens after I book?

You'll receive a welcome pack with packing list, travel logistics, and recommended training within 48 hours, followed by a planning call with your trip lead about 8 weeks before departure.

What's your cancellation policy?

Deposits are refundable up to 90 days before departure. Within 90 days, payments can be transferred to a future trip. Full terms are provided when you book.
